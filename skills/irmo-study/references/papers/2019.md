# IrMO 2019 · 11 May 2019

## Q1 | Quasi primes | number-theory

Define $q_1=2$. For $n\ge2$, the $n$th quasi-prime $q_n$ is the smallest integer greater than $q_{n-1}$ not of the form $q_iq_j$ for $1\le i\le j\le n-1$. Determine with proof whether $1000$ is a quasi-prime.

## Q2 | Seven day sports schedules | combinatorics

Jenny attends a sports camp for seven days. Each day she plays exactly one of hockey, tennis or camogie. In every four consecutive days she must play all three sports. Find with proof the number of possible schedules.

## Q3 | Trapezium angle bisectors | geometry

In quadrilateral $ABCD$, $AB\parallel DC$ and $|BC|=|AB|+|CD|$. Prove the bisectors of $\angle ABC,\angle BCD$ intersect at right angles on side $AD$.

## Q4 | Rational expression zero set | algebra

Find all quadruples $(x,y,z,w)$ of nonzero real numbers satisfying
$$1+\frac1x+\frac{2(x+1)}{xy}+\frac{3(x+1)(y+2)}{xyz}+\frac{4(x+1)(y+2)(z+3)}{xyzw}=0.$$

## Q5 | Reflected circumcentres | geometry

$M$ lies on side $BC$ of triangle $ABC$. $P,Q$ are the circumcentres of $ABM,ACM$. Let $L=BP\cap CQ$ using extended lines, and let $K$ be the reflection of $L$ in line $PQ$ (so $PQ$ is the perpendicular bisector of $KL$). Prove $M,P,Q,K$ are concyclic.

## Q6 | Smallest simultaneous power and consecutive sum | number-theory

The number $2019$ is both (a) the sum of fourth powers of five distinct positive integers; (b) the sum of six consecutive positive integers. Indeed,
$$2019=1^4+2^4+3^4+5^4+6^4=334+335+336+337+338+339.$$
Prove it is the smallest number satisfying both properties. You may assume the displayed equalities.

## Q7 | Symmetric fourth powers | algebra

Nonzero real $a,b,c$ satisfy $a+b+c=0$, $a^4+b^4+c^4=128$. Determine all values of $ab+bc+ca$.

## Q8 | Parallelogram circle intersection | geometry

$G$ lies inside parallelogram $ABCD$. A circle $\Gamma$ through $A,G$ meets $AB,AD$ again at $E,F$, respectively. Lines $FG,EG$ extended meet $BC,CD$ at $H,I$, respectively. The circumcircle of $HGI$ meets $\Gamma$ again at $M\ne G$. Prove $M$ lies on diagonal $AC$.

## Q9 | Cubic constraint inequality | inequalities

Real $x,y,z$ satisfy $x^2+y^2+z^2+2xyz=1$. Prove $8xyz\le1$, with equality exactly at
$$\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right),\quad\left(-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right),\quad\left(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\right),\quad\left(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\right).$$

## Q10 | Island hopping directed graph | combinatorics

There are $64$ islands numbered $1$ through $64$. A holiday visits $a,b,c$ on its three nights, where $b\in T_a$, $c\in T_b$. Each $T_i$ is nonempty, $i\notin T_i$, and $\sum_{i=1}^{64}|T_i|=128$. Exhibit sets $T_i$ giving at least $63\cdot64+6=4038$ holidays. Return to the first island ($c=a$) is allowed. Holidays are distinct if their ordered triples differ in any entry.
