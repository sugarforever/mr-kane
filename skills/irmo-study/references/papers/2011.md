# IrMO 2011 · 7 May 2011

## Q1 | Linear systems | algebra

Suppose $abc\ne0$. Express in terms of $a,b,c$ the solutions $x,y,z,u,v,w$ of
$$x+y=a,\quad z+u=b,\quad v+w=c,\quad ay=bz,\quad ub=cv,\quad wc=ax.$$

## Q2 | Triangle medians | geometry, inequalities

Let $ABC$ be a triangle with side lengths $a=|BC|$, $b=|CA|$, $c=|AB|$. Let $m_a,m_b,m_c$ be the lengths of the medians from $A,B,C$ to the midpoints of their opposite sides.

(a) Prove $2m_a<b+c$. Deduce $m_a+m_b+m_c<a+b+c$.

(b) Give examples of (i) a triangle in which $m_a>\sqrt{bc}$; (ii) a triangle in which $m_a\le\sqrt{bc}$.

## Q3 | Recurrence common factors | number-theory, sequences

The integers $a_0,a_1,\ldots$ are defined by $a_0=1$, $a_1=3$ and $a_{n+1}=a_n+a_{n-1}$ for $n\ge1$. Find all integers $n\ge1$ for which $na_{n+1}+a_n$ and $na_n+a_{n-1}$ share a common factor greater than $1$.

## Q4 | Nested incircles | geometry

The incircle $C_1$ of triangle $ABC$ touches $AB,AC$ at $D,E$, respectively. The incircle $C_2$ of triangle $ADE$ touches $AB,AC$ at $P,Q$, and intersects $C_1$ at $M,N$. Prove:

(a) The centre of $C_2$ lies on $C_1$.

(b) The four points $M,N,P,Q$ in appropriate order form a rectangle if and only if twice the radius of $C_1$ is three times the radius of $C_2$.

## Q5 | Talent show scores | combinatorics

In “The $X^2$-factor”, each of $8$ judges awards each contestant $0$ (fail), $X$ (pass), or $X^2$ (pass with distinction). Ann was awarded the same score as Barbara by exactly $4$ judges. David declares he obtained different scores to Ann from at least $4$ judges and different scores to Barbara from at least $4$ judges. In how many ways could scores have been allocated to David, assuming he is telling the truth?

## Q6 | Nonintegral sum | number-theory

Prove that
$$\frac23+\frac45+\cdots+\frac{2010}{2011}$$
is not an integer.

## Q7 | Tournament parity | combinatorics, number-theory

In a tournament with $N<10$ players, each player plays every other player once, scoring $1$ for a win and $0$ for a loss. There are no draws. Only one player ended with an odd number of points and was ranked fourth. Determine whether this is possible. If so, how many wins did that player have?

## Q8 | Rectangle areas | geometry

$ABCD$ is a rectangle. $E$ lies between $A,B$ on $AB$, and $F$ lies between $A,D$ on $AD$. The areas of triangles $EBC,EAF,FDC$ are $16,12,30$, respectively. Find the area of triangle $EFC$.

## Q9 | Symmetric inequalities | inequalities, algebra

Suppose $x,y,z>0$ and $1=2xyz+xy+yz+zx$. Prove:

(i) $\frac34\le xy+yz+zx<1$;

(ii) $xyz\le\frac18$.

Using (i) or otherwise, deduce $x+y+z\ge\frac32$, and derive the case of equality in this last inequality.

## Q10 | Exponential Diophantine equation | number-theory

Find with proof all solutions in nonnegative integers $a,b,c,d$ of
$$11^a5^b-3^c2^d=1.$$
