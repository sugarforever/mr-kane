## Q1 | Quadrilateral area bound | geometry, inequalities

Points $A,B,C,D$ lie on four different sides of a square of side $r$, and $a=|AB|$, $b=|BC|$, $c=|CD|$, $d=|DA|$. Prove
$$\operatorname{area}(ABCD)\ge r^2-\frac14(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2),$$
and that $ABCD$ is a rectangle in the equality case.

## Q2 | Powers and the minimum exponent | number-theory

Find with proof all positive integer solutions of
$$2^a+2^b+2^c+2^d=60\min\{a,b,c,d\}.$$

## Q3 | Sixty degree angle bisector | geometry

Triangle $ABC$ satisfies $|AB|>|AC|$ and $\angle BAC=60^\circ$. Its circumcentre is $O$, and the bisector of $\angle BAC$ meets $BC$ at $D$. Prove $\angle ADO=2\angle DAO$.

## Q4 | Reachability by digit operations | number-theory

Starting with a positive integer, one may perform any sequence of:

A: Divide by $2$ if it is even.

B: Append the digit $0$.

C: Append the digit $8$.

For example $8\to4\to2\to1\to18\to9\to90$ uses AAACAB.

(a) Prove $2026$ can be reached starting from $8$.

(b) Can every positive integer be reached starting from $8$?

## Q5 | Nonintersecting triangle configurations | combinatorics, number-theory

For positive $n$, label $3n$ distinct points on a circle $1,2,\ldots,3n$ clockwise. An $n$-triangle configuration partitions them into $n$ triples, joining each triple to form a triangle, with no two triangles intersecting. Let $S(n)$ count configurations. Thus $S(1)=1$ and $S(2)=3$, from $\{123,456\}$, $\{234,561\}$, $\{345,612\}$. Find all positive $n$ for which $S(n)$ is not divisible by $3$.

## Q6 | Three pile stone game | combinatorics

Aisling and Brendan play with $2026$ stones.

- Aisling divides them into three nonempty piles.
- Brendan removes one stone from each of two piles, adds one of those stones to the third pile and discards the other.
- They continue alternating, starting with Aisling, making the same kind of move.
- A pile may become zero and may receive stones again, but no pile may become negative.
- The first player unable to move loses.

Determine a strategy ensuring Aisling wins.

## Q7 | Reversed digits divisible by 121 | number-theory

If $\overline{ABCD}$ and $\overline{DCBA}$ are four-digit numbers both divisible by $121$, prove $A+C=B+D$.

## Q8 | Odd set partition obstruction | number-theory, combinatorics

For every positive integer $n$, prove it is impossible to partition $S=\{1,3,5,7,\ldots,2n+1\}$ into disjoint subsets so that in each subset one element equals the sum of all the other elements.

## Q9 | Integer functional equation parameter | algebra

Let $c\in\mathbb Z$. Find all $f:\mathbb Z_{>0}\to\mathbb Z$ such that, for integers $x>y>0$,
$$f(x-y)+f(x+y)=2f(x)+cf(y).$$

## Q10 | Circle intersection line through orthocentre | geometry

Triangle $ABC$ has circumcentre $O$. Lines $BO,AC$ meet at $J$ and lines $CO,AB$ at $L$. The circumcircle of $BOC$ meets line $AO$ a second time at $P$. The circumcircles of $PCJ,PBL$ meet a second time at $Q$. Prove $PQ$ passes through the orthocentre $H$ of $ABC$.
